\chapter{Literature Review}

\section{Stereo Matching Fundamentals}
% Sources: tier1/surveys/Scharstein_Taxonomy_IJCV2002.md,
% tier1/surveys/Hirschmuller_SGM_TPAMI2007.md, papers/CONCEPTS.md,
% review_paper/sections/02_background.tex.
Stereo matching recovers depth from two images of the same scene taken
from horizontally separated viewpoints. The geometry that makes this
possible is the epipolar constraint: a scene point observed in one view
must lie, in the other view, on a known line determined by the relative
pose of the two cameras. Rectification warps both images so that these
epipolar lines become horizontal scanlines at the same row, after which
every pair of corresponding points lies on the same scanline and the
search for a match reduces to a one-dimensional search along it, as
Figure \ref{fig:stereo_geometry} illustrates. The horizontal shift
between a point and its match is the disparity, and it determines depth
through the calibrated pinhole relation
\begin{equation}
Z = \frac{f\,B}{d}
\label{eq:depth_from_disparity}
\end{equation}
where $Z$ is the depth of a scene point, $f$ the camera focal length in
pixels, $B$ the baseline separating the two cameras, and $d$ the
disparity, the horizontal shift of the point between the two rectified
views. Because the relation is inverse, disparity resolution matters most
for distant surfaces: a fixed sub-pixel disparity error spans a growing
depth interval as the scene recedes, which is why the field measures
accuracy in fractions of a pixel.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{fig_1_1_stereo_geometry_preview}
\caption{\textbf{Two-view stereo geometry.} A scene point $P$ projects to $x_L$ and $x_R$ on two rectified image planes separated by the baseline $B$; the disparity $d = x_L - x_R$ recovers depth through $Z = fB/d$.}
\label{fig:stereo_geometry}
\end{figure}

The central intermediate structure is the
disparity space image, or cost volume, which stores for every pixel and
every candidate disparity a score measuring how well the two views
agree; this structure is the direct precursor of the learned cost
volumes used throughout this thesis. Scharstein and Szeliski
\cite{scharstein2002taxonomy} organized every stereo algorithm into four
steps, shown in Figure \ref{fig:classical_pipeline}: matching cost
computation, cost aggregation, disparity optimization, and disparity
refinement. Local methods place their effort
in aggregation, imposing smoothness implicitly through the support
window, and are fast but blur edges; global methods place it in an
explicit energy minimization and are sharp but slow. The classical
matching costs trade robustness against cost, from the illumination
sensitive squared and absolute differences to the gain-invariant
normalized cross-correlation and the illumination-robust census
transform, which encodes the ordering of local intensities rather than
the intensities themselves. The refinement step contributes the
operations that survive into modern pipelines in learned form: sub-pixel
interpolation by fitting a parabola to the cost minimum, a left-right
consistency check that flags occluded pixels by matching in both
directions, and median filtering and hole filling for the flagged
regions.
Semi-global matching \cite{hirschmuller2008sgm} became the classical apex
by approximating a two-dimensional global optimization as a sum of
independent one-dimensional dynamic-programming problems along several path
directions, with the smoothness penalties of \eqref{eq:sgm_energy}, and it
remains the explicit bridge to deep stereo: the first learned method kept
it as an optimization backend, and later networks replaced its fixed
aggregation with learnable layers.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_2_1_classical_preview}
\caption{\textbf{The classical stereo pipeline.} The four canonical steps of the Scharstein and Szeliski taxonomy \cite{scharstein2002taxonomy}: matching cost computation, cost aggregation, disparity optimization, and disparity refinement.}
\label{fig:classical_pipeline}
\end{figure}

Semi-global matching minimizes an energy that sums a per-pixel matching
cost and two smoothness penalties over neighboring pixels, given by
\begin{equation}
E(D) = \sum_{p} C(p, D_p)
  + \sum_{p} \sum_{q \in N_p} \Big[
      P_1\, \mathbb{1}\!\left[\,|D_p - D_q| = 1\,\right]
    + P_2\, \mathbb{1}\!\left[\,|D_p - D_q| > 1\,\right] \Big]
\label{eq:sgm_energy}
\end{equation}
where $D$ is the disparity map, $C(p, D_p)$ the matching cost of assigning
disparity $D_p$ to pixel $p$, $N_p$ the neighbors of $p$, $P_1$ a small
penalty for one-pixel disparity changes that preserves slanted surfaces,
$P_2$ a larger penalty for jumps beyond one pixel that permits true
discontinuities, and $\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$ the indicator function.

\section{The Deep Learning Transition}
% Sources: tier2/end_to_end/{MC-CNN,DispNetC,GCNet}.md,
% tier1/surveys/Poggi_Synergies_TPAMI2021.md.
Deep learning entered stereo matching first through the matching-cost
step. MC-CNN \cite{zbontar2016mccnn} replaced only the cost computation
with a Siamese convolutional network that scores the similarity of two
patches, and fed those scores into an otherwise classical pipeline of
cross-based aggregation, semi-global optimization, and refinement. It
established the enduring trick of computing each image's features once and
reusing them across all disparities, but it was not end-to-end. DispNetC
\cite{mayer2016sceneflow} removed the classical stages entirely and
regressed disparity directly with an encoder-decoder network. Its central
component was a correlation layer, which forms a compact cost volume by
correlating the two feature maps along the scanline, as in
\eqref{eq:correlation}, and it ran roughly a thousand times faster than
MC-CNN at comparable accuracy. The same work introduced the SceneFlow
synthetic dataset, which remains the universal pretraining set for stereo
networks. GC-Net \cite{kendall2017gcnet} completed the transition to a
fully learned cost-volume network. It builds a four-dimensional cost
volume by concatenating the left and right feature vectors at each
disparity, regularizes it with a three-dimensional convolutional
encoder-decoder, and regresses a continuous disparity with the
differentiable soft-argmin of \eqref{eq:softargmin}, an operator that is
still universal today.

The correlation layer forms a scalar matching cost by taking the inner
product of the two feature maps at each disparity along the scanline,
\begin{equation}
c(x, y, d) = \big\langle\, f_L(x, y),\; f_R(x - d,\, y) \,\big\rangle
           = \sum_{k} f_L^{(k)}(x, y)\, f_R^{(k)}(x - d, y)
\label{eq:correlation}
\end{equation}
where $c(x, y, d)$ is the matching cost for reference pixel $(x, y)$ at
disparity $d$, $f_L$ and $f_R$ the shared feature maps, and the inner
product runs over the feature channels $k$; the search covers
$d \in [0, D_{\max}]$.

The soft-argmin turns the regularized cost volume into a continuous
disparity by taking a softmax-weighted expectation,
\begin{equation}
\hat{d}(x, y) = \sum_{d=0}^{D-1} d \cdot
  \operatorname{softmax}_d\!\big(-c_d(x, y)\big)
\label{eq:softargmin}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{d}(x, y)$ is the regressed sub-pixel disparity and $c_d(x, y)$
the aggregated cost at disparity $d$; the negative sign turns low cost
into high probability. The operator assumes a single-peaked cost
distribution and returns an intermediate value where that assumption
fails, such as at a depth discontinuity.

\section{The 3D Cost-Volume Era}
% Sources: tier2/end_to_end/{PSMNet,GWCNet,GANet,AANet,ACVNet,CFNet}.md.
The years that followed refined the three-dimensional cost-volume
paradigm. PSMNet \cite{chang2018psmnet}, shown in Figure
\ref{fig:psmnet}, added spatial pyramid pooling, to inject global context
at the feature level, and three stacked hourglass modules with
intermediate supervision; it became the de-facto baseline for the era.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_2_4a_psmnet}
\caption{\textbf{PSMNet: the 3D cost-volume paradigm.} Architecture overview reproduced from \cite{chang2018psmnet}: spatial pyramid pooling on shared features, a concatenation cost volume, and 3D CNN regularization followed by disparity regression.}
\label{fig:psmnet}
\end{figure}

GwcNet \cite{guo2019gwcnet} introduced the group-wise correlation
volume, which splits the features into channel groups and computes an
inner-product correlation per group, as in \eqref{eq:gwc_lit}. This form
sits between the information-poor full correlation and the expensive
concatenation, and it degrades far more gracefully than concatenation
under a reduced aggregation budget, which is why nearly every later
efficient method adopts it; the tile initialization of this thesis adopts
it as well. GA-Net \cite{zhang2019ganet} attacked the cost of
three-dimensional convolution directly, replacing it with a differentiable
semi-global aggregation layer that approximates recursive aggregation
along a few directions and a local guided aggregation layer for thin
structures, and showed that a network with only two such layers can
outperform GC-Net with nineteen three-dimensional convolutions. Later work
pushed efficiency further: AANet \cite{xu2020aanet} removed
three-dimensional convolution entirely in favor of deformable intra-scale
and cross-scale aggregation, ACVNet \cite{xu2022acvnet} used an
attention map derived from correlation to filter a concatenation volume,
and CFNet \cite{shen2021cfnet} shifted the objective toward cross-domain
generalization through a fused multi-scale volume and an
uncertainty-driven cascade that adaptively narrows the disparity search.

The group-wise correlation splits the $N_c$ feature channels into $N_g$
groups and computes a normalized inner-product correlation within each
group,
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{gwc}}(g, d, x, y) = \frac{N_g}{N_c}\,
  \big\langle\, f_L^{(g)}(x, y),\; f_R^{(g)}(x - d,\, y) \,\big\rangle
\label{eq:gwc_lit}
\end{equation}
where $g$ indexes the group, $d$ the disparity, and $f_L^{(g)}$,
$f_R^{(g)}$ the channel slice of each feature map belonging to group $g$;
the resulting volume carries one channel per group. The tile
initialization of this thesis uses this operator at a single coarse scale,
as \eqref{eq:gwc} makes explicit.

\section{Iterative Refinement}
% Sources: tier1/iterative/* + reference_impls/raft_stereo, igev_stereo.
A different paradigm emerged with RAFT-Stereo \cite{lipson2021raftstereo},
shown in Figure \ref{fig:raftstereo}, which adapted the iterative
refinement of RAFT optical flow to stereo. It builds an all-pairs
one-dimensional correlation volume, uses a separate context encoder to
seed a multi-level convolutional gated recurrent unit, and refines the
disparity through many lightweight update iterations, each of which looks
up correlation values around the current estimate. It restores full
resolution with a learned convex upsampling that predicts a weighted
combination of a coarse neighborhood.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_2_4b_raftstereo}
\caption{\textbf{RAFT-Stereo: the iterative paradigm.} Architecture overview reproduced from \cite{lipson2021raftstereo}: a dedicated context encoder seeds a convolutional gated recurrent unit that iteratively looks up an all-pairs correlation volume.}
\label{fig:raftstereo}
\end{figure}

Three of these mechanisms,
the group-wise cost lookup, the recurrent update, and convex upsampling,
reappear in modified form in the model of this thesis. IGEV-Stereo
\cite{xu2023igev} improved the paradigm by building a geometry encoding
volume, a lightweight three-dimensional-regularized cost volume that both
provides a warm-start disparity through soft-argmin and is looked up each
iteration alongside the all-pairs correlation, as in \eqref{eq:gev_lit};
with this warm start it matches RAFT-Stereo's thirty-two iterations in
three. The geometry encoding volume is the direct ancestor of the geometry
branch used here. The warm start matters because an iterative method spends its early
iterations recovering what a good initialization provides for free; with
it, IGEV-Stereo at a single iteration already outperforms RAFT-Stereo by
a wide margin, and the auxiliary loss on the initial estimate forces the
geometry volume to be a genuine disparity predictor rather than a mere
feature bank. Two later works refined the recurrent core in directions
relevant to this thesis. CREStereo \cite{li2022crestereo} introduced a
cascaded recurrent refinement whose adaptive group correlation layer
searches a small two-dimensional window rather than assuming a perfectly
horizontal epipolar line, and its ablation showed local correlation
outperforming the all-pairs form outright; both findings matter here,
the first because a two-dimensional local search is precisely what
confers tolerance to imperfect rectification, the second because it
justifies replacing the all-pairs volume with a small local lookup in a
compact model. Selective-Stereo \cite{wang2024selective} added a
selective recurrent unit that runs two gate branches with different
kernel sizes and fuses them per pixel through a contextual attention
map, reaching the quality of many more iterations in a few; notably,
when the same unit was tried in the ablation campaign of this thesis it
did not transfer to the compact tile setting, a result Chapter 4
reports.

The recurrent update is a convolutional gated recurrent unit that updates
a hidden state from a per-iteration input,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
z_n &= \sigma(\mathrm{Conv}([h_{n-1}, x_n])), \qquad
r_n = \sigma(\mathrm{Conv}([h_{n-1}, x_n])),\\
\tilde{h}_n &= \tanh(\mathrm{Conv}([r_n \odot h_{n-1}, x_n])), \qquad
h_n = (1 - z_n)\odot h_{n-1} + z_n \odot \tilde{h}_n
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:convgru_lit}
\end{equation}
where $x_n$ assembles the correlation lookup, the encoded disparity, and
the context, $h_{n-1}$ is the previous hidden state, $z_n$ and $r_n$ the
update and reset gates, $\tilde{h}_n$ the candidate state, $\sigma$ the
sigmoid, and $\odot$ the element-wise product; a small head then predicts
the disparity residual from $h_n$.

The geometry encoding volume regularizes a group-wise correlation volume
and combines it with the all-pairs correlation into a lookup structure,
\begin{equation}
C_G = R\big(C_{\mathrm{gwc}}(f_{L}, f_{R})\big), \qquad
d_0 = \sum_{d=0}^{D-1} d \cdot \operatorname{softmax}_d\!\big(C_G(d)\big)
\label{eq:gev_lit}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathrm{gwc}}$ is the group-wise correlation of the
quarter-resolution features, $R$ a lightweight three-dimensional network,
$C_G$ the regularized geometry volume, and $d_0$ the warm-start disparity;
each iteration then looks up values from both $C_G$ and the all-pairs
correlation.

A parallel line replaced the cost volume itself with attention. STTR
\cite{li2021sttr} treats matching as dense sequence-to-sequence
correspondence along the epipolar lines, alternating self-attention
within each image and cross-attention between the views, with an
optimal-transport assignment that handles occlusion explicitly and
removes the fixed maximum-disparity assumption altogether. The
transformer family improved occlusion reasoning and freed the disparity
range, but its dense attention has remained costly at the resolutions
and budgets that edge deployment demands, which is why the proposed
model draws on the recurrent family instead.

\section{Foundation-Model Era}
% Sources: tier1/foundation_model/* + review_paper section 03.
The most recent and most accurate systems share a common recipe: they
graft a frozen monocular vision foundation model onto an iterative stereo
core and train on very large data. FoundationStereo
\cite{wen2025foundationstereo} fuses a frozen DepthAnythingV2 transformer
with a light convolutional encoder through a side-tuning adapter, adds an
attentive hybrid cost filter, and trains on a million synthetic pairs,
reaching 0.34 pixel end-point error on SceneFlow. DEFOM-Stereo
\cite{jiang2025defom} adds a monocular depth foundation model to a
RAFT-Stereo core and splits each iteration into a scale update that
corrects the monocular prior and a standard refinement. MonSter
\cite{cheng2025monster} couples an IGEV stereo branch with a monocular
branch through bidirectional refinement, and Stereo-Anywhere
\cite{bartolomei2025stereoanywhere} uses a monocular prior to truncate the
stereo cost volume and suppress the false matches that mirrors and glass
produce. The details of how the prior enters differ instructively. DEFOM-Stereo
splits each recurrent iteration into a scale update, which corrects the
monocular prior's per-pixel scale through a dedicated lookup over
candidate scale factors, and a standard residual update; its ablation
shows the monocular initialization alone hurts and only becomes useful
with the scale correction. MonSter runs the two branches symmetrically,
each guiding the other after a global scale and shift alignment, and its
ablation shows four iterations with the prior matching what the plain
stereo core needs thirty-two iterations to reach. The recipe therefore
buys its accuracy twice over, from the prior's knowledge of monocular
structure and from the iterations it saves. These systems set the
accuracy ceiling, but they do so with hundreds of millions of parameters
and hundreds of milliseconds of latency, which motivates rather than
solves the edge problem this thesis addresses. Figure \ref{fig:timeline}
traces this progression across the six paradigm eras and marks where the
present work sits.

\section{Efficient and Edge-Deployable Stereo}
% THE load-bearing section (~7 pages). Skeleton: review_paper section 04

The efficient and edge-deployable methods are the closest relatives of the
model proposed in this thesis, and they are best understood by the
technique family each uses to reduce cost. Figure \ref{fig:taxonomy}
organizes these families, and the subsections below treat each in turn.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_2_5_taxonomy_preview}
\caption{\textbf{Taxonomy of compression techniques for deep stereo matching.} Seven technique families organized by the pipeline stage they compress, each with representative methods; this tree organizes the survey of Section 2.6.}
\label{fig:taxonomy}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Backbone Substitution}
The simplest way to reduce cost is to replace the heavy feature encoder
with a mobile backbone. MobileStereoNet
\cite{shamsafar2022mobilestereonet} extended MobileNet's separable
blocks into the three-dimensional cost-volume regularization itself and
offered two design points: a two-dimensional variant at 1.14 pixel
end-point error with 2.23 million parameters, and a three-dimensional
variant at 0.80 pixels with 1.77 million, an order of magnitude smaller
than the contemporaneous volumetric baselines at comparable accuracy.
The lesson generalizes beyond MobileNet: any well-pretrained compact
backbone can serve, and a detection backbone brings the additional
benefit of features already trained to localize objects across scales.
The model of this thesis follows this route, using a truncated
detection backbone as its shared encoder, a choice validated against
two alternatives in Chapter 4.

\subsection{Cost-Volume Compression}
The three-dimensional cost volume is the dominant cost in most stereo
networks, and several families compress it. Cascade methods
\cite{gu2020cascade} build a coarse volume over the full disparity range
and then progressively narrow the range at finer scales; on PSMNet the
two-stage cascade cut memory substantially while improving the end-point
error from 0.887 to 0.721 pixels, showing that range narrowing is not
only cheaper but also more accurate. BGNet \cite{xu2021bgnet} learns a
bilateral grid that upsamples a one-eighth-resolution cost volume in an
edge-preserving way, with a parameter-free slicing step, at 25
milliseconds per frame. DeepPruner \cite{duggal2019deeppruner} predicts a
per-pixel disparity range with a differentiable PatchMatch and a
confidence-range predictor, so that the expensive aggregation runs only
on a pruned set of hypotheses; its fast variant reaches 0.97 pixel
end-point error at 61 milliseconds. HITNet \cite{tankovich2021hitnet},
shown in Figure \ref{fig:hitnet}, removes the three-dimensional cost
volume altogether: it represents the scene as a grid of small tiles, each
carrying a slanted-plane hypothesis with a disparity, two slopes, and a
descriptor, and refines these hypotheses by two-dimensional propagation
and warping across scales, reaching 0.43 pixel end-point error at 0.97
million parameters and 54 milliseconds. The slanted plane lets a tile
represent sub-pixel structure that a fronto-parallel hypothesis cannot,
which is what allows the network to stay accurate without a dense volume.
This tile-hypothesis representation, including its plane slopes, is the
paradigm from which the model of this thesis is built.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_2_4c_hitnet}
\caption{\textbf{HITNet: the tile-hypothesis paradigm.} Architecture overview reproduced from \cite{tankovich2021hitnet}: the scene is a grid of slanted-plane tiles refined by propagation across scales, without a dense 3D cost volume. This is the paradigm the proposed model builds on.}
\label{fig:hitnet}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Iterative-Loop Compression}
Where the iterative methods spend their cost is the update loop, and the
efficient variants compress it. LightStereo \cite{guo2025lightstereo}
replaces three-dimensional aggregation with a two-dimensional network of
inverted-residual blocks that expand the disparity channels, reaching 0.73
pixel end-point error at 3.44 million parameters and 17 milliseconds; its
authors observe that feature extraction, not aggregation, dominates the
remaining runtime. Distill-then-Prune \cite{pan2024dtp} designs a backbone
that is compatible with deployment toolchains by construction, avoiding
three-dimensional convolution and trilinear interpolation, and reaches
1.56 pixel end-point error at 0.26 million parameters with 16 milliseconds
of latency on an embedded board.

\subsection{Knowledge Distillation}
A distinct family reduces the accuracy cost of a small network by training
it against a large teacher. LiteAnyStereo \cite{jing2025liteanystereo},
whose network is shown in Figure \ref{fig:liteany}, pairs a 7.6 million
parameter non-iterative network with a three-stage training pipeline:
supervised training on synthetic data, self-distillation under input
perturbation, and distillation from a frozen FoundationStereo teacher on
unlabeled real pairs. The result is strong zero-shot accuracy that the
same network cannot reach by supervised training alone.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_2_4d_liteanystereo}
\caption{\textbf{LiteAnyStereo: the efficient, distillation-trained paradigm.} Architecture overview reproduced from \cite{jing2025liteanystereo}: a compact non-iterative network that reaches strong zero-shot accuracy through the three-stage training pipeline of Figure \ref{fig:kd_stages}, not through architectural iteration.}
\label{fig:liteany}
\end{figure}

Distill-then-Prune \cite{pan2024dtp} shows that distilling before pruning,
and using the teacher output alone rather than the teacher combined with
ground truth, gives the best student. The common form of the distillation
term is a masked regression between the student and teacher disparity,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KD}} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|}
  \sum_{i \in \Omega} m_i \cdot
  \operatorname{smooth}_{L_1}\!\big(\hat{d}_S(i) - \hat{d}_T(i)\big)
\label{eq:kd_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{d}_S$ and $\hat{d}_T$ are the student and teacher disparities,
$\Omega$ the image domain, and $m_i$ an optional teacher-confidence mask.
This three-stage pipeline, illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:kd_stages}, is
the largest untapped lever for the model of this thesis, and it is
revisited as future work in Chapter 5.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_2_7_kd_stages}
\caption{\textbf{The three-stage knowledge-distillation pipeline of LiteAnyStereo.} Reproduced from \cite{jing2025liteanystereo}: supervised synthetic training, self-distillation with perturbation, and fine-tuning on unlabeled real data with pseudo labels from a frozen accurate model.}
\label{fig:kd_stages}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Architectural Compression and Adaptive Compute}
Two further families reduce cost through architecture and through
adaptivity. CoEx \cite{bangunharcana2021coex} showed that a correlation
volume, which is cheap but information-poor, can be re-enriched from the
image side: its guided cost-volume excitation derives per-channel
attention weights from the left features with inexpensive convolutions
and applies them to the volume at every aggregation scale, recovering
the accuracy of the far more expensive concatenation volume at 2.72
million parameters and 27 milliseconds. It also replaced the full
soft-argmin with a regression over the top disparity candidates, which
avoids the between-modes averaging of Section 2.2. CGI-Stereo
\cite{xu2023cgistereo} extended the idea by fusing context into geometry
at every decoder scale with bidirectional gradient flow, beating far
larger networks zero-shot at a fraction of their cost. The lesson, that
cheap image-derived guidance can substitute for volumetric capacity,
recurs in this thesis's guided excitation and mask-based upsampling.
LightStereo \cite{guo2025lightstereo}, treated above under loop
compression, contributes a complementary finding: expanding the
disparity channels inside its inverted-residual aggregation blocks
helps, while expanding the spatial kernels does not, locating the useful
capacity along the disparity axis rather than the spatial one.
Adaptive-compute methods change the cost at run time. AnyNet
\cite{wang2019anynet} produces a usable disparity after a first coarse
stage and refines it in later stages through residual cost volumes with a
narrow search range, so an embedded board can trade 29 milliseconds of
coarse depth against 97 milliseconds of refined depth as the time budget
allows. MADNet \cite{tonioni2019madnet} attacks the domain gap at run
time instead: it adapts online to a new environment by updating one
network module per frame under a photometric self-supervised loss, with a
bandit strategy choosing which module to update, and recovers most of the
accuracy that a zero-shot deployment loses while sustaining 25 frames per
second. The residual narrow-range search of the former and the
coarse-to-fine budget of both are visible ancestors of the local lookup
and iteration schedule used in this thesis.
Finally, neural architecture search has been applied to the volumetric
pipeline: LEAStereo \cite{cheng2020leastereo} jointly searches the feature
and matching networks under the structure of the stereo pipeline and
reaches 0.78 pixel end-point error at 1.81 million parameters, evidence
that carefully allocated small budgets rival hand designs an order of
magnitude larger.

Three lessons emerge from the efficient family taken as a whole, and they
shape the design of Chapter 3. First, the cost volume is where the budget
goes, and every successful compact method either shrinks it, prunes it,
or replaces it with a sparser hypothesis structure; the tile
representation is the most aggressive of these and the one adopted here.
Second, image-derived guidance is cheap and effective: the excitation,
bilateral-grid, and mask-based upsampling mechanisms all use the image to
steer a small volume or a coarse prediction, and the convex upsampling
and context stream of this thesis follow that pattern. Third, the
training recipe is not an afterthought; the distillation results show
that the same small architecture can land at very different accuracy
depending only on how it is trained, which is why this thesis treats its
training protocol as a first-class design object and ablates it with the
same rigor as the architecture. These families are largely complementary,
and several can be combined, a point the model of this thesis exploits by
pairing a substituted backbone with a compressed cost volume and a
compressed recurrent loop.

\section{Cross-Domain Generalization}
% Sources: tier3/domain_shift/*, review_paper section 05.
A network trained on synthetic data often fails on real domains, because
it learns shortcut features tied to the synthetic statistics, such as
consistent local color distributions, rather than domain-invariant
structure, and it breaks in textureless, reflective, repetitive, and
large-disparity regions. The magnitude is large: GA-Net trained on
synthetic data and evaluated zero-shot reaches 11.7 percent outliers on
KITTI 2015 and 32.2 percent on Middlebury, and PSMNet reaches 16.3
percent on KITTI, several times their fine-tuned rates. Two families of
remedy exist. Architecture-level methods make the features themselves
domain-invariant. DSMNet \cite{zhang2020dsmnet} adds a domain
normalization that removes image-level and pixel-level statistic shifts
together with a structure-preserving graph filter, and improves the
zero-shot KITTI rate from 11.7 to 6.5 percent and the Middlebury rate
from 32.2 to 21.8 percent. HVT \cite{chang2023hvt} applies hierarchical
visual transformations at global, local, and pixel level during training
only, at zero inference cost, and pushes the zero-shot KITTI rate further
to 4.9 percent on the same backbone. Training-recipe methods change the
data and the supervision instead: StereoAnything
\cite{guo2024stereoanything} mixes labeled stereo with tens of millions
of monocular-to-stereo pseudo-pairs and shows that dataset diversity
matters more than dataset size, and LiteAnyStereo
\cite{jing2025liteanystereo} reaches strong zero-shot accuracy with a
non-iterative network trained by three-stage distillation. The relative importance of iteration and training pipeline
for generalization is not settled in the literature. Some efficient works
argue that iteration is necessary for cross-domain accuracy, while
LiteAnyStereo reaches competitive zero-shot numbers without any iteration,
and the cleanest controlled evidence attributes its accuracy to the
training pipeline rather than to any recurrent module. This thesis returns
to the question in Chapter 4, where the training pipeline is shown to close
most of the cross-domain gap for a non-iterative tile network.

\section{Benchmarks and Datasets}
% Sources: tier2/datasets/*, review_paper section 02.3.
The benchmarks that this thesis uses are summarized in Table
\ref{tab:datasets}. SceneFlow is a large synthetic dataset of 35,454
training pairs across three subsets with dense rendered ground truth, and
it is the standard pretraining set; only its FlyingThings3D subset
provides an official test split, which is used here for in-domain
evaluation. Its value lies in the density and exactness of its labels,
which real sensors cannot provide, at the price of a domain gap that
Section 2.7 discussed. KITTI 2012 and 2015 provide real driving scenes
with sparse LiDAR ground truth, and their D1-all outlier rate has become
the field's headline metric for driving. ETH3D covers mixed indoor and
outdoor scenes at gray-scale with laser-scanned ground truth. Middlebury
2014 provides high-resolution indoor scenes with dense structured-light
ground truth, and it is the hardest of the four in two respects: its
images reach several megapixels, so most methods evaluate at half or
quarter resolution, and its disparities extend to several hundred pixels,
far beyond the search range most networks are built for. These two
properties make it the standard zero-shot stress test, and it serves as
the cross-domain benchmark of this thesis. Its evaluation protocol splits
the scenes into a training set with public ground truth and a test set
whose ground truth is withheld on a server; the zero-shot convention
followed here, and by the reference methods this thesis compares against,
evaluates on the ground-truth-public scenes without any training or
fine-tuning on Middlebury. The metrics themselves also carry conventions
worth noting: end-point error rewards sub-pixel accuracy on smooth
surfaces and can look healthy while boundaries are poor, whereas the
bad-$t$ and D1-all rates count outlier pixels and expose exactly the
boundary and textureless failures that end-point error averages away.
For this reason the results of Chapter 4 always report both families of
metric together.

\section{Summary and Research Gap}
Table \ref{tab:method_comparison} and Figure \ref{fig:pareto} summarize
the trade-offs across the eras. The accuracy frontier is held by the
foundation-scale and iterative systems, at hundreds of millions and tens
of millions of parameters respectively, while the efficient family
occupies the low-parameter region at some cost in accuracy. Within that
efficient region, the surveyed literature does not directly address the
specific combination this thesis targets: a tile-hypothesis network of a
few million parameters that fuses a geometry encoding volume through a
fail-soft gate, is measured for near real-time inference on the Jetson
Orin Nano class of hardware, and is characterized for robustness to
imperfect rectification. The cross-domain evidence surveyed above further suggests
that the training pipeline, more than architectural iteration, governs
zero-shot accuracy for compact networks, a point the following chapters
take up directly.
