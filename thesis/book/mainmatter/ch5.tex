\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}

\section{Summary of Findings and Contributions}
This thesis set out to build an edge-deployable deep stereo matching
network under two objectives, and both were met. The first objective, a
computationally efficient pipeline with AI-based disparity refinement for
resource-limited platforms, is answered by the trained model: StereoLite
uses 2.96 million parameters, reaches 0.78 pixel end-point error on the
SceneFlow test set and 10.9 percent D1-all zero-shot on Middlebury 2014,
and runs in 49.8 milliseconds in half precision on a laptop RTX 3050, with
a projection of roughly 30 milliseconds on the Jetson Orin Nano class.
The second objective, tolerance to imperfect rectification, is answered by
the perturbation study of Section \ref{sec:rectification}: accuracy is
retained across the sub-pixel to one-pixel vertical misalignment that a
calibrated rig produces, with the end-point error rising only from 1.03 to
1.53 pixels, owing to the local correlation lookup in the refinement
stage.

The contributions of this thesis are the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item We present StereoLite, a 2.96 million parameter stereo network that
combines a truncated YOLO feature encoder, a left-image recurrent context
stream, plane-aware coarse-to-fine tile refinement, and a fail-soft
quarter-resolution geometry encoding volume, designed for the edge budget.
\item We show that a controlled training pipeline, combining native-crop
sampling, augmentation, and full-dataset training, closes the cross-domain
gap of the tile-hypothesis family, reducing the zero-shot Middlebury 2014
D1-all rate from 40.1 percent on an earlier chassis to 10.9 percent at a
comparable parameter count.
\item We provide an equivalence-proven inference-optimization pass that
reduces latency by a factor of 1.74 with no loss of accuracy, and we
identify the operators that block eight-bit deployment.
\item We achieve 0.78 pixel end-point error on the SceneFlow test set with
real-time inference on a laptop GPU, and we demonstrate zero-shot transfer
to an unseen benchmark and to a real camera without adaptation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
The present work has several limitations, stated plainly. The Jetson Orin
Nano latency is a projection from the measured laptop-GPU figure, not a
direct on-device measurement, because the hardware was not available. The
results come from a single training run, so run-to-run variance is not
characterized. A gap remains on cross-domain accuracy: at 10.9 percent
zero-shot D1-all the model is well behind the distillation-trained
LiteAnyStereo at 6.9 percent and the iterative IGEV-Stereo at 5.0 percent.
The maximum disparity is capped at 192 pixels at the inference resolution,
supervision is synthetic only, and the model has not been submitted to the
KITTI leaderboard. Architecturally, the final recurrent reasoning stops at
one quarter resolution, which limits the recovery of thin and distant
structures, and the network carries no explicit left-right consistency or
occlusion reasoning. The rectification tolerance measured in Chapter 4
holds only for small vertical offsets and does not extend to large
de-rectification.

\section{Future Work}
Several directions follow from these limitations. The most promising is a
three-stage knowledge-distillation pipeline in the manner of
LiteAnyStereo, in which the model is first trained on synthetic data, then
self-distilled under perturbation, and finally distilled from a foundation
teacher on unlabeled real pairs. The cross-domain evidence of Chapter 4
points to the training pipeline as the largest remaining lever, and this
model's supervised-only recipe leaves that lever unused. A second
direction is eight-bit export to TensorRT and a direct latency measurement
on the Jetson Orin Nano, which would replace the projected figure with a
measured one; the operators that block export were already identified in
Section 3.12. A third direction is to complete the zero-shot evaluation
quartet with KITTI and ETH3D, so that generalization is characterized
across driving and mixed domains rather than the indoor domain alone. A
fourth is a temporal extension that propagates the tile-plane state across
video frames, which the plane representation is well suited to carry. We
believe that a compact tile-hypothesis network trained with the
distillation pipeline above will be useful as an off-the-shelf edge stereo
component, and establishing that is the natural next step of this work.
