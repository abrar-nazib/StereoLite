\chapter{Thesis Management and Finance}

\section{Introduction}
This chapter records the schedule, allocation of resources, and
expenditure associated with the thesis. The paper collection and
literature review took place during the fourth-year odd semester (the
seventh semester); the main project period then ran from December 2025
to July 2026.
Model training and experimental studies used rented cloud GPUs. The
project also acquired a stereo camera and a Jetson Orin Nano for data
acquisition and deployment testing. Section 6.2 describes the management
of the work and its seven phases, Section 6.3 presents the overall
expenditure, and Section 6.4 separates the hardware, cloud-computing, and
software costs.

\FloatBarrier
\section{Management of the Thesis}
The work was divided into phases with defined outputs, and some phases
deliberately overlapped so that activities could run in parallel. The
literature review guided the selection of candidate models, and the setup
and preliminary training that followed established the experimental
pipeline. Real-data capture continued while the architecture was refined
through ablation studies. The selected model then went through
benchmarking, three-dimensional reconstruction, and final evaluation
before the results were consolidated in the thesis. This overlap let data
preparation, experimentation, and writing proceed together while design
decisions still depended on the experimental evidence that supported
them.

\section{Component/Software Budget}
The budget in Table \ref{tab:expenses} splits into hardware, cloud
computing, and software.

Hardware covered the stereo camera and the Jetson Orin Nano, used for
real-data acquisition and on-device testing, at a combined cost of about
BDT 35,000 (USD 280). Both count as development hardware for validating
the pipeline, not as the deployed system itself.

Cloud computing cost about USD 140 in total: roughly USD 100 on A100
instances for the main training run, and about USD 40 on other GPU
instances for ablation studies, intermediate checks, and final
evaluation. These are usage totals accumulated over the whole development
period, not the rate for a single day, and they will not match a
provider's current hourly tariff since prices vary by accelerator,
region, and storage allocation.

The software stack, described in Chapter 7, uses open-source tools and
cost nothing in licence fees. Cloud use was limited to development;
inference runs locally on the Jetson device, so deployment adds no
recurring per-frame cloud charge, only ordinary hardware upkeep and
power.

\FloatBarrier
\section{Summary}
This chapter summarized the management and finances of the thesis. The
main schedule ran from December 2025 to July 2026, following the
literature review conducted in the preceding semester, and consisted of
seven overlapping phases from model setup to final documentation. Total
expenditure was about USD 420 (BDT 52,500): USD 140 for cloud GPU usage
and USD 280 for the stereo camera and Jetson Orin Nano. Using
open-source software meant no licence fees, and because inference runs
locally on the Jetson device, deployment does not add a recurring cloud
charge.
