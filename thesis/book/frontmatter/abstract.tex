\chapter*{Abstract}

Mobile robots, drones, and augmented reality devices need real-time
depth from stereo cameras, and they must compute it within a power
budget of a few watts. The most accurate deep stereo networks cannot meet
that budget: they rely on foundation-scale backbones of hundreds of
millions of parameters, which embedded hardware cannot hold or run in
real time. This thesis presents StereoLite, a stereo matching network of
2.96 million parameters that combines slanted tile-plane hypotheses with
recurrent disparity refinement. A group-wise correlation volume at one
sixteenth resolution initializes a tile state; a dedicated left-image
context encoder then feeds convolutional GRU updates that refine the
state across three scales. A narrow-band geometry encoding volume adds
long-range corrections through a learned fail-soft gate, and
plane-equation rendering with learned convex upsampling recovers sharp
full-resolution disparity. The network is trained only on synthetic
SceneFlow data and reaches 0.78 px end-point error and 3.40 percent
D1-all on the full FlyingThings3D test set of 4,370 pairs. On the unseen
Middlebury 2014 benchmark it scores 10.9 percent D1-all zero-shot, and
one forward pass at $384 \times 640$ takes 49.8 ms on a laptop RTX 3050
GPU in half precision. On the target Jetson Orin Nano, the optimized
eight-bit model runs in 36.3 ms, or 27.5 frames per second. The network
also tolerates the imperfect rectification of a low-cost stereo rig:
under a controlled vertical misalignment sweep, end-point error rises
only from 1.03 to 1.53 px across the sub-pixel to one-pixel range that a
calibrated rig produces. The predicted disparity back-projects through
the calibrated rig geometry into metric colored point clouds on real
indoor scenes. The project repository
(\url{https://github.com/abrar-nazib/StereoLite}) archives the training
code, the evaluation drivers, and the trained checkpoint so that every
reported number can be reproduced.
