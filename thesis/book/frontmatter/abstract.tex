\chapter*{Abstract}

Real-time depth perception from stereo cameras is a core requirement for
mobile robots, drones, and augmented reality devices that must operate
within a power budget of a few watts. However, the most accurate deep
stereo networks rely on foundation-scale backbones with hundreds of
millions of parameters, placing them far beyond the reach of embedded
hardware. This thesis presents StereoLite, a 2.96 million parameter
stereo matching network that combines slanted tile-plane hypotheses with
recurrent, AI-based disparity refinement. A group-wise correlation volume
at one sixteenth resolution initializes a tile state that convolutional
GRU updates refine across three scales, guided by a dedicated left-image
context encoder. A narrow-band geometry encoding volume proposes
long-range corrections through a learned fail-soft gate, and
plane-equation rendering with learned convex upsampling recovers sharp
full-resolution disparity. Trained only on synthetic SceneFlow data, the
network attains 0.78 px end-point error and 3.40 percent D1-all on the
full FlyingThings3D test set of 4,370 pairs. Zero-shot evaluation on the
unseen Middlebury 2014 benchmark yields 10.9 percent D1-all, and
inference at $384 \times 640$ resolution takes 49.8 ms on a laptop
RTX 3050 GPU in half precision. Projections indicate roughly 30 ms on
Jetson Orin Nano class hardware after INT8 optimization; these projected
values await confirmation on the physical device. All training code,
evaluation drivers, and the trained checkpoint are archived in the
project repository to support full reproduction of every reported number.
